# Coast Rush

Coast Rush is Play Simi's original three-lap race around Palm Bay, on a drift route with three hairpins, a long carousel, two sweepers and esses along the south shore. Eight karts share the grid: the saved player avatar and seven named game characters, or up to eight players racing each other live. The coastal course, scenery, kart and simulation are Play Simi's own work. No Tidal Rush art, name, code or audio is included. KayKit characters remain credited in `art/characters/CREDITS.md`.

## Playing

- W/up or the touch GO button accelerates. A/D or left/right steers. S/down brakes. On the keyboard the throttle is held. A tap on GO latches the throttle until BRAKE, so a tap, a short hold and a long press all drive; the long-press browser menu is suppressed over the race.
- The race clock runs for every active second after the countdown, even with no throttle. Pause or P, losing focus, and backgrounding stop the clock until Resume. Slow rendering still advances the same 60 replayable physics ticks per active second. TIME LEFT shows seconds remaining in the two-minute limit.
- Until a player's first finished race, the kart steers itself back from the road edge when the player is not steering. The player's own steering always wins, and the help ends with the first finish. It only shapes the inputs sent to the server; physics, replay checks and the rules version are unchanged.
- On touch screens, steering sits under the left thumb and a 2×2 pad (ITEM, BRAKE, DRIFT, GO) under the right. Holding DRIFT or ITEM also holds the throttle, and a thumb can slide between neighbouring buttons without lifting, so drifting needs one right-hand finger. The cover and prompts use touch wording on touch devices.
- Hold Space or DRIFT while moving, then release for Mini (0.8 seconds held), Super (1.6 seconds) or Ultra (2.4 seconds). The meter names the available tier. Turbos last 0.8, 1.4 or 2.1 seconds respectively.
- The route is built for drifting. Normal steering holds the two sweepers at cruising speed. The three hairpins (radius about 14 to 16 units against a 10.8-unit road) throw a kart that is not drifting out onto the sand, and into the wall from a wide line; a drift holds the road and charges a turbo on the way round. The carousel is long enough to charge an Ultra. At turbo speed every bend needs the drift.
- A drift looks like one: the kart hops as it starts, swings its nose into the bend and holds the angle, the front wheels counter-steer, the body rolls, tyre marks stay on the road and smoke comes off the rear wheels. Sparks appear once a turbo is earned and change colour with the tier: blue for Mini, orange for Super, pink for Ultra. Rivals slide through tight bends too. With reduced motion the kart still angles and marks the road, without the hop, roll, smoke or sparks.
- The cover explains crates, the seven items and shells before the race.
- Crates sit in four rows a lap, three across, on the straights. Each gives one of seven items at random; E or ITEM uses the current item once per press, and holding E cannot use a subsequently collected item.
  - **Shield**: blocks bumps and item hits for three seconds.
  - **Turbo**: a two-second speed burst.
  - **Missile**: homes on the next kart ahead in the race and spins it. With no kart ahead it flies off.
  - **Storm**: every kart ahead drops to 55% of its pace for three seconds.
  - **Coconut**: dropped just behind the kart. The first other kart to reach it spins. A driver can steer round one; computer karts cannot. It never affects the kart that dropped it.
  - **Wave**: spins every kart within 14 units along the road, ahead or behind.
  - **Crab**: scuttles up the road after the race leader and spins them. With no kart ahead it flies off.
- A spin lasts 1.25 seconds: the kart turns twice round and rolls on at a crawl with no steering. It never stops dead.
- The draw mixes the run's seed with the tick the crate was collected on, so it differs from race to race but the server replays the same item. The leader has no kart ahead, so draws only shield, turbo, coconut or wave. There are no purchases.
- Computer karts fight. Each takes about two crate rows in five and uses the item within a second and a half, on whoever it applies to: other computer karts and players alike. An item waits for a target: a missile, storm or crab for a kart ahead, a coconut for a kart within 25 units behind, a wave for a kart in range, a shield for an incoming missile or crab. After four seconds without one, a shield or coconut is used anyway and anything else is let go. With scripted drivers a race puts twenty to thirty rival items on the road and hits the player five or six times. The HUD names the kart and the item.
- A computer kart's pickup shows: the crate it collected from disappears for about a second and then returns (a driver can still collect it), and the kart's nameplate carries the item's icon until it is used or let go. This is drawing only. A computer kart collects from the outer crate on its own side of the road, never the middle one.
- Computer karts also drive at other karts. About two-thirds of the time, in spells of a second and a half, a computer kart moves across the road to block a faster kart within 9 units behind it, or leans on a kart alongside. It never does this to a kart that is shielded or spinning. At other times it pulls out to pass a slower kart ahead, gives a kart alongside room, and returns to its lane.
- In a first race (before a first finish) rivals leave the crates alone and do not block or lean. Karts are still solid.
- Three teal boost pads a lap, before the carousel, into the esses and on the home straight. Shells are optional track pickups, up to 36 across three laps. They are counted at the finish and do not grant speed, currency or medal bonuses. The HUD shows the running count out of 36.
- Nothing stops a kart. Touching the wall takes about a fifth of its speed and the drift charge, and rubbing along it holds the kart to about 70% of its pace until it steers away. Sand slows cruising a little. Repeated knocks have a short cooldown.
- Karts are solid, to drivers and computer karts alike. A kart counts as 1.8 units long and 1.35 wide. One that runs into the back of another is held behind it and slides off to one side; the kart in front is nudged forwards and not slowed. Door to door, the two push each other apart, so a kart can be leaned on and moved off its line, as far as the wall. Meeting a kart takes about a seventh of a driver's speed (a tenth of a computer kart's), and for as long as two karts touch each is held to 80% of its pace. Staying in contact is one knock and then a rub, not a knock every moment. A shielded kart keeps its line and its speed and the other kart gives way.
- The 3, 2, 1 countdown precedes the race clock. Place is always shown as, for example, 3rd / 8, and all eight karts appear on the minimap.
- Marina, Reef, Sunny, Pebble, Mango, Breeze and Coco form the street ladder. Every rival is labelled Game character in the scene and results. Fixed, staggered paces make a first-lap pass possible without drifting. They never create player accounts, shared challenges or medal entries.
- Results show place, total time, best lap, shells and the number of bumps. A first completed race or an improvement in personal time or place displays New best. Another round starts in the same page. A two-minute unfinished race records No finish, earns the normal completion cosmetic reward, and cannot set a racing best or rank.
- Pause, loss of focus and hidden tabs freeze the simulation and clear controls. Simple graphics uses the same race rules. Graphics context loss pauses the race and allows resuming in the map view.

## Racing friends live

- On the cover, **Race friends live** opens a lobby. **Host a race** gives a four-letter code; friends enter it under **Have a code?**. Two to eight players can join, and computer karts fill the rest of the grid to eight. Only the host can start, and can start the next race from the results.
- Everyone races the same three laps at once. Players line up ahead of the computer karts, two abreast. Other players appear in their own avatars with their names; the minimap rings their dots. Items, bumps and the finishing order are shared: a missile fired at the player ahead spins that player.
- The clock is shared, so a multiplayer race cannot be paused. **Leave** offers the way out while the race carries on. A player who leaves, or loses their connection, takes their kart off the road and the others race on.
- Results list every kart by finishing time, then by distance for those that ran out of time.
- Players in a room can chat in the lobby, during the race and on the results. **Chat** opens eight fixed lines ("Good luck!", "Nice drift!", "One more?" and so on); on a keyboard the number keys 1 to 8 say them without opening anything. The last four lines show above the item button for eight seconds each. There is nowhere to type: only the line's number is sent (`shared/race-chat.ts`), the room passes on one line per player every 1.5 seconds, and nothing is stored. **Mute chat** hides everyone's lines on that player's screen.
- These are friendly races. They give no XP, medals, Play medals, records or ranked times, and nothing from a room is saved. The room exists only while someone is in it.
- How it works: each browser drives its own kart with the same physics as a solo race and reports its position about fifteen times a second. The room (`shared/race-room.ts`) owns the computer karts, every item in flight and the finishing order, keeps each reported kart on the road and moving at a plausible speed, and sends everyone the same picture, including the item each computer kart holds. Contact follows the same split: each browser holds its own kart off the others, and the room does the same for the computer karts, against positions that are a few frames old. Blocked players cannot see or join each other's rooms. On Cloudflare a room is one `Race` Durable Object named by its code, held in memory; the Node preview serves the same rooms over a WebSocket and needs the dev dependencies installed (`ws`).

## Sound and the finish

- Sound is on by default for new players, and starts with their first tap, as browsers require. Players who already have a saved profile keep their existing setting. The race cover and footer each have a sound button; it changes the same saved setting as the top-bar toggle.
- With sound on, the race has its own music, which speeds up on the final lap, and a continuous engine, tyre-squeal and wind loop that follows speed, drifting and turbo. Effects cover the countdown, shells (rising in pitch when collected in a run), crates, shield on and off, drift tiers, turbo, hits, position changes, laps, the final lap, the last ten seconds, the flag and a time-limit ending. Everything is synthesised; there are no audio files.
- Collecting a crate, using a shield and starting the final lap also show on screen. The shield bubble pulses and blinks through its last second, and the screen edge glows while it is active.
- Crossing the line starts a finish sequence of up to nine seconds: the kart rolls to a stop, the camera swings round to the driver, and place, time, best lap and shells appear in turn. See results, or Enter, skips it. Rivals that have finished park beyond the line. The clock stops at the line, so the sequence never changes a time.
- The result is sent to the server when the line is crossed, not after the sequence, so closing the window during it does not lose the round.

## First visit and accounts

Age and privacy consent still come first. Play now opens Coast Rush directly. Before the first completed round, the neighbourhood's shop, hunt, camera tools and extra menus are hidden and walking is paused. Sound, accessibility settings and account/privacy controls remain accessible. Closing an unfinished race returns to Play now.

Any completed game round, including a zero-score finish, still unlocks the lilac cap and its one-time welcome XP. The first race result reveals the neighbourhood and the other four cabinets. Its main button is Name & customise your character: the wardrobe opens with the lilac cap on and the nickname field ready, then Next: save my character asks for an email and a six-digit code typed into the same window. Maybe later skips either step. On later races a guest's new-best result offers Save your avatar and this score, opening the same email form. Creating an account is optional; guest progress remains saved on the current session. No email is sent without submitting that form.

## Competition and preservation

Verified players compete by fastest completed three-lap time. Street-ladder place and shells never add medal points. Account verification remains; every verified round is ranked immediately, with no practice mode or waiting period. Gold/silver/bronze award 3/2/1 at midnight Singapore time, ties share ranks, and five cabinets allow up to 15 points per day. Missing a day never subtracts points. The existing shared-challenge rules remain unchanged.

Coast Rush now uses replay rules version 5 (solid karts that rub, and rivals that block, lean and use about twice as many items). Version 4 was the drift route with bumps that only slowed, rivals with items, and seven items. The other four games stay on version 1 with unchanged simulation behaviour. Completed race scores remain the exact integer `7201 - finishTicks`; zero is DNF. The server replays inputs to derive time, place, lap and pickup results. It stores personal bests and the run's result transactionally so retries neither duplicate rewards nor lose the original New best flag. Graphics and sound only observe simulation state. The touch, sound and finish changes of 4 October 2026 did not alter `shared/racing.ts`.

A change to the route or rules does alter it, so older times are archived each time exactly as two-lap times were: the runs stay in the database, the previous personal best moves to `coastLegacyBest`, and older times and challenges are excluded from new boards and targets. The server records the first day of the winding route as `coast_route_from` of the drift route as `coast_drift_from`, and of solid karts as `coast_contact_from`; earlier unsettled days still settle against the version that was current then, and on a cutover day only new runs compete. A round started before a change and finished after it is rejected as expired. Because a personal best under new rules starts empty, every player's next race after a change is a first-finish race again: 180 seconds, rivals that ease off, and no rival items or blocking.

Old two-lap runs are retained in the database. The previous personal best is retained as `coastLegacyBest` in progress/export data. Old race times and posted challenges are excluded from the new race boards and targets. Historical awarded medals remain unchanged; unprocessed days before the Singapore cutover day settle against the old race version. On the cutover day, only three-lap runs compete for Coast Rush's pending medals. Other games, saved avatars and cosmetic indices are preserved.

## Checks

Automated coverage includes manual throttle, first-lap passing, all drift tiers, the route's shape and the harbour hairpin with and without a drift, the seeded draw of all seven items, each item's effect, rivals using items (and not in a first race), a spin that never stops the kart, a replay that uses items, one-use input edges, shield protection, bumps that only slow, solid karts (a driver held behind a kart and sliding off it, two karts door to door, computer karts against each other, and no two karts ever sharing a piece of road), computer karts blocking (and not in a first race), replay determinism, tampering, DNF, personal records, retry handling, two-lap, old-loop, winding-route and version 4 score migration, midnight medals and the shared challenge limit. The Cloudflare test completes a three-lap race and checks its persisted result and real-player board entry.

Multiplayer coverage (`tests/race-room.test.ts`) includes room codes, the grid, seating limits, blocked pairs, host-only start, clamping of reported positions, the message rate limit, item hits and shield blocks between players, computer karts' items in a room, finishing order, leaving, the time limit and a second window replacing the first. Two players race over real sockets against the Node preview server, and again against the Cloudflare runtime in `tests/cloudflare.test.mjs`.

Browser testing uses isolated local data and synthetic guest accounts. The multiplayer check of 10 October 2026 was one desktop Chrome window racing a scripted second player on the same machine: it shows the lobby, live karts, items and results working, not how the race feels over a real network or on a phone. Desktop phone-size checks do not establish performance or touch feel on a physical iPhone.
